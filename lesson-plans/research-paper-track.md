# Research Paper Track (One Paper per Student)

## Purpose

Each student will complete one research paper on a technology topic of their choice to build technical reading, synthesis, and communication skills.

## Recommended parameters

- Length: minimum of 1,024 words.
- Sources: minimum 4 credible sources (at least 2 primary/official sources).
- Format: MLA or APA (choose one and apply consistently).
- Submission: `.pdf` plus source list file in student repo.

## Suggested topic list (students may propose alternatives)

- How search engines rank content.
- Cybersecurity basics for everyday users.
- How video game physics engines work.
- Ethical risks of AI-generated content.
- Cloud computing vs. on-premises infrastructure.
- The evolution of programming languages.
- How open-source software communities operate.
- Data privacy and personal digital footprints.

## Milestone integration into semester

## Week 4: Topic proposal

- `paper_topic_proposal.md`:
  - topic,
  - why chosen,
  - one guiding question.

## Week 6: Preliminary bibliography

- At least 4 sources with one-sentence credibility note per source.
- Mini-lesson: evaluate sources with the CRAAP test (Currency, Relevance,
  Authority, Accuracy, Purpose) before adding them.

## Week 8: Thesis and outline

- 1 thesis statement.
- 5-7 section outline.

## Week 11: First draft (50% complete)

- Intro + at least two body sections.

## Week 13: Peer review workshop

- Each student gives feedback to two peers.
- Feedback template: clarity, evidence, structure, citation quality.

## Week 15: Full draft

- Complete draft with citations and references.

## Week 17: Final paper submission

- Final paper (`.pdf`) and source list due, so Week 18 is presentation-only and
  the last week is not overloaded.

## Week 18: Mini-presentation

- 3-minute summary presentation per student on the paper submitted in Week 17.

## Rubric (suggested)

- 30% Argument quality (clear claim and logic).
- 25% Evidence and source quality.
- 20% Organization and writing clarity.
- 15% Citation/format accuracy.
- 10% Revision effort across milestones.

## Anti-AI authorship safeguards

- Require in-class writing checkpoints (thesis, outline, paragraph draft).
- Require annotated bibliography notes in student voice.
- Require oral defense: each student explains one claim and one source choice.
- Keep revision history through GitHub commits.
