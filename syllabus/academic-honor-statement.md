# Academic Honor Statement — Introduction to Coding (Python)

## Why this statement exists

This course is designed to help you build **real, authentic coding ability**.
Those skills only grow when the work is genuinely your own. This statement is a
promise — to yourself, your classmates, and your instructor — that you will do
your own thinking and be able to explain everything you turn in.

You will sign this in **Week 1** and keep it for the full semester.

## My commitments as a student

By signing below, I agree that for the entire duration of this course:

1. **I will do my own work.** All homework, projects, and research-paper
   milestones will be written by me, in my own words and my own code.
2. **I will not submit AI-generated code or content.** I understand that using
   AI tools to generate my assignments is not allowed in this course.
3. **I can explain everything I submit.** If asked, I will walk through any part
   of my work and describe how and why it works.
4. **I will keep an honest commit history.** I will commit my work progressively
   through GitHub so my process is visible, rather than uploading everything at
   the last minute.
5. **I will ask for help the right way.** I may ask my instructor, classmates,
   and approved course resources for guidance and debugging help, but the final
   work will be my own.
6. **I understand how authorship is checked.** Commit history and short in-class
   code walkthroughs are used to confirm that I understand my work.
7. **I understand the consequences.** Any submission I cannot explain may be
   reassessed in class, and repeated violations follow the school's academic
   integrity policy.

## What counts as allowed help

To keep expectations clear:

**Allowed**

- Asking the instructor or a classmate to help you understand an error message.
- Reading official documentation, course handouts, and approved resources.
- Discussing ideas and approaches, then writing your own code.
- Using a search engine to learn a concept.

**Not allowed**

- Pasting an assignment prompt into an AI tool and submitting its output.
- Copying another student's code and presenting it as your own.
- Turning in work you cannot read, run, or explain.
